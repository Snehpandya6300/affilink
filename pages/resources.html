Guide 01

How to choose an affiliate program worth your traffic.

Start with relevance, not payout. The highest commission in the directory is worthless if your audience doesn't buy the product. Filter by the category your content already lives in, then shortlist three to five programs you could genuinely recommend.

  • Product-market fit first. Would you mention this product in a normal post without being paid?
  • Conversion reality. A 10% commission on a product that converts at 4% can beat a 30% commission on something nobody finishes checkout for.
  • Program stability. Prefer programs with clear terms, a named network and a track record of paying affiliates.

Use the directory filters to shortlist by category and commission type, then open the detail page and read the requirements before you apply.

Guide 02

Revenue share, recurring, CPA and tiered: what actually pays.

Revenue share pays a percentage of the sale. Best for higher-ticket products where one conversion is meaningful.

Recurring commission pays a percentage of subscription revenue for a defined period — often 12 or 24 months. For SaaS, a 20% recurring commission usually beats a one-off 30% share once a customer stays past the first renewal.

Fixed CPA pays a flat amount per signup or first order. Predictable, and usually best when the product has a low price but strong conversion.

Tiered structures increase the rate with volume or plan level. Worth pursuing if you already send consistent traffic.

Rough check: estimated monthly conversions × payout = expected value. Compare that number across programs, not the headline percentage.
Guide 03

Cookie duration and why 30 days is not the same as 90.

A cookie window is how long your referral is remembered after someone clicks your link. A 7-day cookie is fine for an impulse purchase. It is almost useless for a B2B tool with a two-month evaluation cycle.

For SaaS and higher-priced products, prefer 60–120 day cookies. For consumer products, 14–30 days is normal. Bear in mind that ad blockers, privacy settings and cross-device journeys can still break attribution — no cookie window is perfect.

Guide 04

Keep your applications organised.

Most affiliate frustration is admin, not content: which programs did you apply to, when, and what did they say? Use the creator dashboard to save programs, log an application and update its status as you hear back.

  1. Save programs you're seriously considering.
  2. Apply through the official network page, then track it here with the date.
  3. Update the status to pending, approved, rejected or withdrawn as responses arrive.
  4. Review your approved list quarterly and drop programs that no longer convert.
Guide 05

Disclosure and trust.

Always disclose affiliate relationships clearly and near the link, not buried in a footer. Prefer products you've actually used. Both are required by most affiliate networks and by consumer protection rules in many countries.

Affilink is a discovery layer: we list program terms and send you to the official application page. We do not approve applications, track your commissions or pay you — the brand and its network do that.
